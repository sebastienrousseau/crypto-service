01 Executive Summary: The Illusion of Distance

For the past five years, enterprise software architects treated post-quantum cryptography (PQC) as an academic exercise—a theoretical migration scheduled for some vague point in the mid-2030s. That luxury expired in August 2024 when the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) released final standards for FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA), followed by draft FIPS 206 (FN-DSA).

Simultaneously, the US National Security Agency's CNSA 2.0 timeline established non-negotiable enforcement milestones: post-quantum algorithms must begin mandatory deployment across software and operating systems by 2027, progressing to web browsers, network devices, and cloud services by 2030. In Europe, the European Banking Authority (EBA) and European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority (EIOPA) under DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) have begun scrutinizing cryptographic legacy risk, while the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) binds software manufacturers to enforceable vulnerability and cryptographic update baselines.

The Acute Threat: Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later (HNDL)

Nation-state actors are systematically capturing and storing encrypted enterprise traffic (SWIFT transactions, database snapshots, ISO 20022 wire feeds, client identity tokens). Any confidential payload with a regulatory or operational lifespan exceeding three years is already compromised if encrypted solely via RSA-2048, ECDSA, or classical Diffie-Hellman / X25519.

This report delivers a rigorous assessment of Shor's algorithm qubit scaling, comparative trade-offs of lattice vs. hash primitives, a competitive war-gaming against legacy and institutional players, and a comprehensive 10-pillar inspection of the 18 packages comprising the @sebastienrousseau/crypto-service monorepo.

02 The 2027 Quantum Horizon & Regulatory Convergence

2.1 The Collapsing Qubit Threshold: 10,000 Physical Qubits

The classical consensus long assumed that breaking 2048-bit RSA or 256-bit elliptic curves would require millions of physical qubits operating under surface-code quantum error correction (QEC). Recent developments in low-overhead error-correcting codes—specifically Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (qLDPC) architectures, transversal gate constructions, and optimized modular exponentiation circuits—have compressed that estimate by nearly two orders of magnitude.

Recent cryptographic research suggests Shor's algorithm could break classical discrete logarithm and factoring problems on architectures with as few as 10,000 to 20,000 physical qubits when utilizing long-range quantum interconnects and catalytic magic-state distillation. When paired with commercial roadmaps from IBM, Google Quantum AI, and Quantinuum targeting tens of thousands of physical qubits with two-qubit gate fidelities exceeding 99.9% before 2029, the operational threshold for cryptographically relevant quantum computers (CRQC) has moved inside the lifecycle of standard enterprise software deployments.

3.1 The Hybrid Strategy: RFC 10024 & Dual Encapsulation

Neither NIST, BSI, nor ANSSI recommend jumping blindly to pure ML-KEM in production without a classical safety net. The recommended enterprise architecture is Composite Hybrid Key Exchange (RFC 10024 / X25519 + ML-KEM-768): 

K = HKDF-Extract(salt, ECDH(sk_classic, pk_classic) || Decap(sk_pq, ct_pq))

Under this dual-construction, an attacker must break both the discrete logarithm problem over Curve25519 and the module lattice problem over ML-KEM simultaneously.

3.2 Memory Hygiene and Constant-Time Execution

Standard JavaScript runtimes present severe side-channel risks: V8's scavenger GC leaves keys lingering in heap buffers, while JIT branch prediction creates timing leaks. In @sebastienrousseau/crypto-wasm, all cryptographic arithmetic executes inside linear WebAssembly memory buffers explicitly zeroized upon completion via volatile overwrites.

06 Security Posture & Vulnerability Remediation

Resolution of qs CVE-2026-82417 & Array Bypass Advisories

Prior to this milestone, Dependabot flagged two medium-severity vulnerabilities in transitive dependencies resolving qs (< 6.16.0):

  • GHSA-4mjr-xmp4-gh2g (CVE-2026-82417): Denial of Service via attacker-controlled isBuffer prototype property causing unhandled process exceptions.
  • GHSA-w7rc-vj24-2rh6: Array limit bypass via bracket-key comma parsing, enabling remote memory exhaustion via sparse array allocations.

Remediation Mechanism: Configured root package.json and pnpm-workspace.yaml overrides locking qs: >=6.16.0. Regenerated pnpm-lock.yaml, systematically purging outdated resolutions and securing all Express and Fastify dependencies.

Audit Result: 100% Clean Security Dashboard
