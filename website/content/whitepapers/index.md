<h2 class="book-title"><a href="/whitepapers/quantum-safe-payments/">Quantum-Safe Payments: Why the Payments Industry Must Act Now</a></h2>
<p class="book-meta"><time datetime="2025-09-01">September 2025</time> · Sebastien Rousseau · <a href="https://emergingpaymentsasia.org/" rel="external noopener">Emerging Payments Association Asia (EPAA)</a></p>
<p class="book-meta book-meta-faint">English · PDF / HTML · 18.9 MB · Free download</p>
<p class="book-excerpt">Quantum computing threatens the cryptographic foundations of financial services. Payments, from real-time to cross-border settlement, rely on protections that quantum computing will eventually render obsolete, and regulators are already treating harvest-now-decrypt-later as a credible present risk. This paper, produced for the Emerging Payments Association Asia, outlines the structural threat post-quantum cryptography poses to payment infrastructure across SWIFT, real-time gross settlement (RTGS) rails and instant payment schemes, and argues for coordinated industry action — starting with cryptographic-asset inventories, PQC migration roadmaps aligned to the NIST FIPS 203/204/205 standards, and crypto-agility built into wholesale payment authentication.</p>

<h2 id="qa-heading" class="qa-headline">Questions? <span class="qa-headline-soft">Answers.</span></h2>
</header>
<section class="qa-list">

<details class="qa-item">
<summary class="qa-q">What kind of research and publications are published here?</summary>
<section class="qa-a">
<p>Two complementary strands sit side-by-side. <strong>Industry white papers</strong>, produced for bodies such as the <a href="https://emergingpaymentsasia.org/" rel="external noopener">Emerging Payments Association Asia (EPAA)</a> and financial standards consortia, examine structural shifts to payment infrastructure — notably the impact of cryptographically-relevant quantum computing on wholesale and real-time gross settlement rails. <strong>Technical architecture blueprints</strong>, developed by Sebastien Rousseau and the Crypto Service core team, describe engineering patterns built on the suite: self-hosted cryptography services, database field encryption, and CBOM generation for cryptographic inventories.</p>
</section>
</details>

<details class="qa-item">
<summary class="qa-q">Who is the intended audience for these publications?</summary>
<section class="qa-a">
<p>Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs), Heads of Payments, Chief Risk Officers, and Principal Enterprise Architects across Tier-1 banks, institutional asset managers, central banks, and market infrastructure operators (SWIFT, Fedwire, RTGS). Each publication assumes cryptographic and financial domain literacy and skips introductory background to focus on architectural trade-offs, formal hardness reductions, and production migration mechanics.</p>
</section>
</details>

<details class="qa-item">
<summary class="qa-q">Are these white papers free to access and distribute?</summary>
<section class="qa-a">
<p>Yes. All institutional white papers, benchmark specifications, and compliance manuals published by Crypto Service are openly accessible for institutional study and architectural evaluation under open documentation norms. EPAA publications are freely downloadable directly from the Emerging Payments Association Asia portal.</p>
</section>
</details>

<details class="qa-item">
<summary class="qa-q">May our risk committee or architecture board cite these papers?</summary>
<section class="qa-a">
<p>Yes. Formal quotations, architectural excerpts, and benchmark figures with attribution are encouraged. For EPAA publications, cite the Emerging Payments Association Asia Quantum-Safe Cryptography Working Group (2025). For Crypto Service technical publications, cite as <em>Rousseau, S. (2026). [Title]. Crypto Service Technical Publications.</em> Canonical DOIs and GitHub permanent commit hashes are provided in each paper's citation block.</p>
</section>
</details>
